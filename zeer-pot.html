Zeer Pot Evaporative Refrigerator

Building an electricity-free clay refrigerator to preserve fruits and vegetables using natural evaporative cooling.

Status

Completed

Tested Location

Gandhinagar

Temp Drop

Core Tech

Evaporative Cooling

Historical Context & Problem

Before modern electricity and refrigeration existed, keeping fruits and vegetables fresh was a huge challenge. High ambient temperatures combined with wild daily temperature swings of 2°C to 40°C caused fresh produce to rot rapidly. To solve this, humans developed the Zeer Pot—an ancient technology based on the same natural cooling principle behind traditional clay water pots (matkas). By storing produce inside an inner chamber surrounded by wet sand, food stays protected at a remarkably stable and cooler temperature.

Build Setup & The Science Behind It

1. Physical Build:
I built the setup using two different sized terracotta flower pots—one small pot placed inside a larger outer pot. The gap between the pots was filled with wet sand, and the top was covered with a custom clay lid (mitti ka dhakkan) to seal the inner food storage chamber.

2. Latent Heat of Vaporization:
Water in the wet sand seeps through the porous walls of the outer clay pot. As warm ambient air passes over the outer surface, the water evaporates. Liquid water needs thermal energy to transition into vapor, so it absorbs heat directly from the inner pot, cooling down the food stored inside.

3. The Impact of Ambient Humidity:
The cooling capability of a Zeer pot is heavily dependent on atmospheric humidity. In dry air, water evaporates rapidly, creating a significant drop in temperature. In high humidity, the surrounding air is already saturated with water vapor, slowing down evaporation and reducing the cooling effect.

Field Testing & Results

  • Testing Site: Tested outdoors in Gandhinagar under local climate conditions.
  • Temperature Drop: Achieved a consistent temperature reduction of 4.5 °C inside the storage chamber relative to the outside air temperature.
  • Thermal Stability: The thick sand layer effectively buffered against external temperature spikes, maintaining a steady internal environment for fresh produce.

Outcome & Reflection

This project successfully proved how effective low-cost, ancient thermodynamics can be for off-grid food preservation. By understanding the balance between water evaporation rate and ambient humidity, simple materials like clay flower pots and sand can provide an eco-friendly, zero-electricity solution to extend the shelf life of fresh food.
