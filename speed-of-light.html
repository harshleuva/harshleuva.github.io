Speed of Light & Li-Fi Audio Transmission

A proposed experimental setup to transmit audio via Li-Fi and measure light travel delay using dual-channel phase shift analysis on a laptop audio card.

Analysis Tool

Phase Delay / $c$

Proposed Concept & Goal

The idea behind this project is to use Li-Fi technology (transmitting sound wirelessly through an LED/laser light beam to a photodetector) and test whether a standard laptop sound card can detect the tiny time delay of light over distance by comparing reference and received audio signals.

Technical Feasibility Analysis

1. Will Li-Fi audio transmission work?
Yes, absolutely. Modulating an LED or laser driver with an analog audio output (like a 3.5mm headphone jack) and picking up the light on a solar cell or photodiode will transmit clear audio without issues.

2. Can a laptop measure the speed of light ($c$) directly via time delay?
Direct time-of-flight measurement is challenging. Light travels at $3 \times 10^8$ m/s, taking only ~3.3 nanoseconds per meter. Standard laptop sound cards record at 44.1 kHz or 96 kHz, giving a time resolution between samples of ~10 to 22 microseconds.

3. How to make it work on a laptop:
Instead of measuring raw time delay, use high-frequency phase shift detection. By sending a high-frequency sine wave (e.g., 15–20 kHz) over a long optical path and comparing the reference signal (Left channel) with the photodetector signal (Right channel) in audio software like Audacity, subtle phase shifts can be measured to calculate the speed of light.

Planned Setup & Workflow

    1. Optical Transmitter: Connect a high-speed LED or diode laser to a driver circuit taking input from the laptop's audio output. 2. Receiver & Photodetector: Connect a fast-response photodiode to a high-speed operational amplifier fed directly into the laptop line-in/microphone jack. 3. Dual-Channel Reference Logging: Split the signal so Channel A records the direct electrical output while Channel B records the light receiver output. 4. Phase Shift Calculation: Vary the optical path distance and calculate the phase angle shift to derive the speed of light.

Planned Next Steps

First build the basic Li-Fi audio circuit to verify clear analog sound transmission over light. Once stable, set up a dual-channel recording rig in Python or Audacity to measure high-frequency phase shifts across varying path lengths.
