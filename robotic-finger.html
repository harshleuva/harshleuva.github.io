Human Robotic Finger

A biomimicry project designed to replicate the movement of a human finger using a simple yet effective string-based tendon mechanism.

Status

Completed

Materials

Wood, Wire, M-Seal

Actuation

Thread Tendons

Payload Capacity

5 kg Load Tested

Project Overview

This biomimicry exploration focuses on replicating biological joint dynamics and flexor tendon routing of human digits. Using hand-drawn structural templates and lightweight composite materials, the robotic finger achieves realistic joint articulation and high structural strength under tension load.

Key Specifications & Features

  • Phalange Structure: Custom hand-drawn and precision-cut wooden components made from reinforced popsicle sticks.
  • Joint Pinning & Framing: Metal wire axles integrated with molded M-Seal epoxy compound for rigid joint alignment.
  • Tendon Actuation System: High-tensile thread routing that mirrors human digital flexor and extensor mechanics.
  • High Strength-to-Weight Ratio: Engineered assembly capable of sustaining up to a 5 kg tensile load without structural failure.

Build & Testing Methodology

    1. Anatomical Sketching & Part Tracing: Drew proportional phalange segment layouts directly onto material stock prior to precision cutting. 2. Joint Fabrication & Molding: Formed pivot points using metal wires, reinforced with M-Seal epoxy to ensure smooth rotational movement and joint stability. 3. Tendon Threading: Routed tensile threads along the ventral side of the finger structure to emulate natural flexion upon pulling. 4. Load & Kinematic Testing: Verified natural articulation curves and conducted tensile strength tests, confirming a maximum holding capacity of 5 kg.

Outcome & Reflection

The assembled digit achieved highly natural motion while surpassing mechanical strength expectations by successfully holding a 5 kg weight. Future iterations can incorporate servo-driven tendon tensioning for full robotic integration and multi-finger robotic hand assemblies.
