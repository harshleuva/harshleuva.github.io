Cornstarch Bioplastic 2.0

Investigating starch gelatinization using cornstarch, vinegar, and glycerine to form bioplastic sheets and testing their mechanical usability.

Base Polymer Cornstarch (Amylose)
Additives Glycerine & Vinegar
Drying Period 2–3 Days
Outcome Formed (Non-Usable)

Materials & Measurements

  • Cornstarch: 10 g
  • Water: 120 mL (Solution 1) + 30 mL (Solution 2)
  • Glycerine: 5 mL
  • Vinegar (Dilute Acetic Acid): 5 mL
  • Mould release agent: Coconut or non-stick oil
  • Mixing vessel, spatula, heat source, flat drying mould

Fabrication Procedure

  1. Solution 1: Mix 120 mL water, 5 mL glycerine, and 5 mL vinegar in a container.
  2. Solution 2: In a separate container, dissolve 10 g cornstarch in 30 mL water.
  3. Heat Solution 1 until it starts simmering, then pour Solution 2 while continuously stirring.
  4. Keep mixing over medium heat until it forms a thick, viscous gel similar to glue.
  5. Pour the mixture into a lubricated mould and let dry undisturbed for 2–3 days.

Thermal Gelatinization Stages

Temperature Range Molecular Stage Structural Transformation
62°C – 72°C Water Absorption Starch granules absorb ambient liquid and begin to swell rapidly.
75°C – 80°C Amylose Leaching Swollen granules rupture, leaching amylose polymers out into the liquid phase.
80°C – 95°C Polymer Entanglement Amylose chains tangle together, forming a microscopic 3D mesh trapping glycerine and vinegar.

Post-Drying Result & Usability Analysis

Practical Result

Rigid & Non-Functional for Bags

The bioplastic sheet successfully formed after 2–3 days of drying, but it turned out stiff and brittle. While a continuous material layer was synthesized, it lacked the flexibility required for real-world applications like bags or packaging.

Scientific Usability Analysis

1. Acid Hydrolysis Impact (Vinegar)

Vinegar breaks down branched amylopectin into straight amylose chains. However, excess acid hydrolysis shortening the polymer chains excessively leads to low structural elasticity upon complete drying.

2. Glycerine Plasticization Efficiency

Glycerine adds flexibility by spacing polymer chains apart. In this cornstarch ratio, the plasticizer wasn't sufficient to offset starch retrogradation, resulting in high surface rigidity rather than smooth flexibility.

3. Retrogradation & Starch Crystallization

During slow drying, amylose chains re-align into rigid crystalline regions. This matrix lock gives the sheet shape but makes it susceptible to cracking when bent, making Agar-agar (Version 1.0) far superior for flexible applications.
