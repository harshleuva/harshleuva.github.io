What is a Terrarium & How Does It Work?

A terrarium is a miniature, self-contained indoor botanical garden housed inside a transparent glass or plastic container. It operates as a functional micro-ecosystem, replicating fundamental Earth processes such as the water cycle, gas exchange (photosynthesis and cellular respiration), and nutrient recycling.

1. Closed Terrariums

Hermetically sealed units that trap moisture. Water transpired by plants and evaporated from soil condenses on the cold glass walls and trickles back down like rain (closed hydrological loop). They require high humidity-loving species like mosses and ferns.

2. Open Terrariums

Containers left open to ambient air currents. Ideal for arid and semi-arid flora such as succulents, cacti, and air plants (Tillandsias) that cannot tolerate high ambient humidity or stagnant air.

Substrate Layering & Mechanical Purpose

01

False Bottom / Drainage Layer (Coarse Gravel / Lava Rock)

Purpose: Prevents root rot. Standing water collects below the root level in the interstitial spaces between coarse rocks rather than saturating the soil matrix.

02

Physical Separation Barrier (Mesh Screen / Fiberglass Grid)

Purpose: Separates fine soil particles from sifting down into the drainage gravel over time, ensuring the false bottom remains open for water pooling without clogging.

03

Activated Charcoal & Organic Barrier

Purpose: Acts as a chemical filter. Activated carbon adsorbs toxins, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and prevents foul odors caused by anaerobic bacterial decomposition.

04

Bioactive Substrate Layer (ABG Soil Mix)

Purpose: Provides anchorage, moisture retention, aeration, and essential macro/micronutrients for plant root systems without compacting under high humidity.

Substrate Mix Recipe & Moss Types

Custom Soil Composition (Atlanta Botanical Garden / ABG Style)

Standard potting soil compacts quickly and suffocates roots in closed high-humidity environments. A tropical bioactive mix consists of:

2 Parts: Coco Coir / Peat Moss
2 Parts: Bark Chips (Orchid Bark)
1 Part: Sphagnum Moss (Milled)
1 Part: Perlite / Pumice
1 Part: Charcoal Granules
0.5 Part: Worm Castings

Moss Species Selection

  • Cushion Moss (Leucobryum glaucum): Forms compact, rounded carpet mounds ideal for hardscape accents.
  • Java Moss / Christmas Moss (Taxiphyllum): Thrives under extreme humidity and direct moisture on rocks and wood.
  • Sheet Moss (Hypnum): Spreads horizontally, providing complete soil coverage to regulate surface evaporation.

Bioactive Cleanup Crew & Water Management

Why Add Springtails (Collembola) & Isopods?

In a closed environment, warm humidity causes mold, fungal spores, and rotting leaf detritus to rapidly multiply. Springtails are tiny hexapods that act as the natural cleaning crew. They consume fungal hyphae, mold spores, and decaying matter, preventing fungal outbreaks without damaging live plant tissues. Isopods (dwarf woodlice) break down larger leaf litter into nutrient-rich castings.

Water Regulation Strategy

Overwatering is the #1 cause of terrarium failure. The total volume of water added during setup should be approximately 5% to 10% of the container volume.

  • Optimal Balance: Minor condensation should form along the glass during early morning and late evening, clearing up during midday.
  • Overwatered Sign: Heavy droplets dripping down all glass walls constantly (Requires opening the lid for 6-12 hours to ventilate).
  • Underwatered Sign: Absence of condensation and dry, pale moss cushions.
