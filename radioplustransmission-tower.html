Project Overview

Indoor FM radio reception was severely degraded due to structural signal attenuation from concrete walls and electromagnetic interference. To solve this, a rooftop half-wave dipole antenna receives clean FM broadcasts and routes them via coaxial cable into a custom-built receiver. The receiver decodes and filters the audio, passing it to an ATtiny85-driven low-power local FM transmitter tower. The tower rebroadcasts the signal across a 10-meter radius on unused frequencies, allowing any commercial radio inside the house to pick up stations cleanly without needing an external wire.

What is an FM Wave & How Do We Capture It?

Frequency Modulation (FM): Unlike Amplitude Modulation (AM) where signal strength varies, FM encodes audio information by varying the instantaneous frequency of a high-frequency carrier wave around a center frequency. The commercial broadcast band ranges from 88 MHz to 108 MHz in the Very High Frequency (VHF) spectrum, with wavelengths (λ) spanning approximately 2.78 meters to 3.41 meters (λ = c / f).

Methods for Capturing FM Signals:

  • Random Wire Antenna: Simple single-wire conductor. Easy to deploy but poorly matched in impedance (~300 Ω+), highly susceptible to stray indoor electromagnetic noise, and directional-deficient.
  • Telescopic Monopole: Common on portable radios. Quarter-wave monopole relying on a ground plane reference; offers moderate reception but lacks precise directional tuning.
  • Half-Wave Dipole Antenna: A resonant two-element balanced antenna matched to half the operating wavelength (λ/2). Provides optimal impedance matching (~73 Ω), strong signal gain, and directional noise cancellation.

Antenna Physics & Coaxial Feedline Selection

1. Half-Wave Dipole Design & Resonant Length Calculation

A dipole consists of two symmetrical metallic conductive elements oriented end-to-end. For the center of the FM band (~98 MHz), the total resonant length L is calculated using the speed of electromagnetic propagation with an end-effect velocity factor multiplier (k ≈ 0.95):

L = (c × 0.95) / (2 × f) = 299,792,458 × 0.95 / (2 × 98,000,000) ≈ 1.45 meters (72.6 cm per element leg)

When passing FM radio waves cross these copper arms, an alternating Voltage/Current standing wave pattern is induced across the center feedpoint, transferring maximum RF energy to the feedline.

2. Why Coaxial Cable (RG-6) instead of Ordinary Wire?

Carrying high-frequency RF signals down from a terrace using standard unshielded parallel wire results in massive signal loss and interference. Coaxial cables solve this through structural geometry:

  • Electromagnetic Shielding: Coaxial cable features a central inner copper conductor wrapped in a dielectric insulator, surrounded by a continuous braided metallic shield and foil layer. The outer shield acts as a Faraday cage, blocking ambient electrical noise from household appliances.
  • Controlled Characteristic Impedance: Manufactured to maintain a continuous 75 Ω characteristic impedance along its length, matching the ~73 Ω natural feedpoint impedance of a dipole antenna to minimize signal reflection (Standing Wave Ratio / SWR losses).
  • Zero Radiation Leakage: The equal and opposite currents flow strictly along the inner conductor and inner surface of the outer shield, containing the RF energy inside without emitting unwanted radiation along the run from the terrace.

Receiver Operations & The Rebroadcast Tower

How the Receiver Processes the Signal: The high-frequency RF signal coming down the coaxial cable enters an RDA5807M IC tuner controlled by an Arduino Nano via I2C protocol. The chip filters out neighboring channel crosstalk using digital signal processing (DSP), converts the RF carrier down to intermediate frequencies (IF), and demodulates the frequency variations back into raw analog audio signals.

Passive Filtering Network: Before reaching the transmitter, the raw analog audio passes through a custom low-pass passive RC filter network. This strips out high-frequency digital noise harmonics generated by the microcontroller and power supply ripple, preventing audio distortion.

How the Local Rebroadcast Transmitter Works: The clean audio signal feeds into an ATtiny85-microcontroller-managed local FM transmitter module. The module modulates this audio back onto a user-selected vacant local frequency (e.g., 87.5 MHz or 107.9 MHz) using a Voltage-Controlled Oscillator (VCO). Attached to a low-power vertical antenna tower, it broadcasts an indoor bubble (~10-meter radius), letting standard household FM radios tune in with crystal clarity.

Key Specifications & Features

  • Custom Antenna Assembly: ~145cm tuned half-wave dipole constructed using PVC pipe casing and copper element wiring mounted on the terrace.
  • Low-Loss Transmission Line: 75-ohm RG-6 coaxial feedline bringing clean RF signals down to the living space with minimal attenuation.
  • Arduino & RDA5807M Core: Microcontroller-managed tuner processing external RF inputs into clean analog audio output.
  • Noise Filtering Circuit: Integrated passive capacitor network designed to clean audio signal lines before local re-transmission.
  • IR Remote & Station Memory: Preset station switching with remote button navigation displayed on a TM1637 4-digit module.
  • ATtiny85 Rebroadcast Unit: Compact low-power FM transmitter module relaying audio over a ~10-meter local radius across empty frequency channels.

Build & Implementation Methodology

    1. Dipole Antenna Fabrication: Built a tuned half-wave dipole element inside a weatherproof PVC support structure on the terrace and connected low-loss RG-6 coaxial cable to bring RF signals indoors. 2. Receiver & Filtering Assembly: Interfaced an RDA5807M receiver module with an Arduino board, integrating a custom capacitor noise-filtering circuit for audio clarity. 3. Control Interface Integration: Connected an IR receiver and TM1637 display module, programming station preset jumping and digital frequency readout. 4. Transmitter & Tower Setup: Configured an ATtiny85 microcontroller with an RF transmitter module to continuously rebroadcast the cleaned audio to indoor receivers on dead frequencies.
