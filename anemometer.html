Cup Anemometer: From Outdoor DIY to Arduino Digital RPM Sensing

Development of a rotational wind speed instrument—evolving from an outdoor outreach DIY build to a custom 3D-printed digital anemometer powered by an Arduino Nano.

Build Evolution

Model 1: Simple DIY anemometer used during online demonstration sessions.

Model 2: 3D-printed 3-cup rotor integrated with Arduino Nano & breadboard circuit.

Sensor Type Rotational Cup Anemometer

Aerodynamic Working Principle & Signal Processing

1. Aerodynamic Drag Asymmetry

A cup anemometer relies on the difference in drag coefficients between the open and closed sides of hemispherical cups. When wind blows across the assembly, the open side facing the airflow experiences significantly higher aerodynamic drag than the convex back side of the opposite cup. This net torque differential drives continuous rotation proportional to horizontal wind velocity.

2. Initial Iteration: DIY Outreach Model

The first prototype was built as a lightweight, low-cost model for online sessions and live teaching demonstrations. Using basic household materials and a vertical spindle, it served as a visual instrument to demonstrate wind drag differential and qualitative speed changes.

3. Digital Upgrade: 3D Printing & Arduino Telemetry

To obtain quantitative measurements, the design was upgraded to a custom 3D-printed 3-cup rotor with balanced mass distribution and smooth axial bearings. A rotational pulse sensor triggered an interrupt pin on the Arduino Nano with every full revolution.

RPM = (Pulse Count / Pulses per Rev) * (60 / Time Interval in Seconds)

4. Velocity Calibration

By combining the measured rotation rate (RPM) with the known radius (R) of the rotor arms, the linear tangential speed of the cup centers is calculated. Multiplying by an empirical anemometer factor (K) accounts for aerodynamic slip and internal friction, yielding direct real-time wind speed in meters per second (m/s).
