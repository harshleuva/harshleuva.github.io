Hair Hygrometer: Keratin Hydration & Mechanical Transduction

An experimental setup utilizing human hair micro-extension driven by relative atmospheric humidity. Investigating keratin conformational changes and mechanical amplification across two iterative prototypes.

Prototype Iterations

Model 1: Blue acrylic frame with wooden base and paper-straw lever pointer.

Model 2: Laser-cut clear acrylic frame, wooden pointer arm, tension pulley, and printed grid.

Active Medium Degreased Human Hair
Mechanism Lever & Pulley Tension
Biochemical Focus Keratin Swelling
Frame Materials Acrylic & Laser-Cut Wood

Materials & Construction

  • Human Hair Strand (Alcohol degreased)
  • Isopropyl Alcohol / Ethanol
  • Model 1: Blue acrylic sheets & wooden slot base
  • Model 2: Clear acrylic frame & baseboard
  • Laser-cut wooden pointer arm & tension block
  • Printed millimeter measurement scale grid

Methodology & Biophysical Principles

1. Chemical Degreasing & Pre-treatment

Untreated human hair is coated in natural lipids (sebum), which forms a hydrophobic barrier that prevents ambient moisture from interacting with the inner cortex. Washing the strand with isopropyl alcohol strips away these surface oils, exposing the hygroscopic matrix to surrounding water vapor.

2. Molecular Mechanism: Keratin Matrix Hydration

Human hair consists primarily of α-keratin, a fibrous protein stabilized by hydrogen bonds and disulfide linkages (S-S). When relative humidity increases, water molecules infiltrate the cortex and break inter-chain hydrogen bonds.

This process relaxes the polypeptide coils, causing the hair strand to expand by roughly 2% to 2.5% across the full humidity range. When humidity drops, water desorbs, hydrogen bonds reform, and the strand contracts.

3. Mechanical Transduction & Amplification

Since the absolute length change (ΔL) is on the order of millimeters, a mechanical lever system amplifies the motion across a graduated scale. The ratio between the long pointer arm (r2) and the pivot distance (r1) determines the amplification factor:

Amplification Factor = r2 / r1

4. Iterative Design: Model 1 vs. Model 2

Model 1: Built using blue acrylic sheets anchored into a slotted wooden base. A paper straw served as the primary lever arm. While functional, structural flex and simple pivot attachment caused friction losses.

Model 2: Upgraded to a rigid, clear acrylic stand with a laser-cut wooden pointer arm. A weighted tension block and thread alignment system kept the hair under consistent axial tension, while a printed millimeter grid allowed precise readout tracking.
