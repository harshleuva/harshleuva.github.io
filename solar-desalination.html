Solar Water Desalination

Using free solar thermal energy to convert highly saline water into ultra-pure drinking water through enclosed evaporation and condensation.

Phase 1: Objective & Build Process

The sun is a free source of light energy that can drive clean water production without requiring electricity. The goal of this experiment was to take extremely salty water and extract pure water using simple household materials.

Build Steps:

    1. Base Container: Took a large metal bowl container and secured a clean, smaller collection bowl right at the center using tape. 2. Saline Solution: Filled the main outer container with heavily salted water (>40,000 PPM), making sure the inner collection bowl stayed completely clean and dry inside. 3. Plastic Seal: Sealed the top of the large container loosely with a clear plastic film, making it airtight to trap maximum heat. 4. Sloped Canopy: Placed a small weight at the center of the plastic cover so it created a downward funnel slope directly pointing over the small inner bowl.

Phase 2: How It Works (The Physics)

By making the system airtight, sunlight creates an internal greenhouse effect that traps heat and raises the temperature far higher than the surrounding ambient air. As the saline solution heats up, pure water evaporates into steam, leaving heavy salt compounds behind. When the rising water vapor hits the cooler plastic sheet above, it condenses into liquid droplets. Gravity guides these droplets along the downward slope to the center, where they drip straight into the collection bowl.

Phase 4: Outcome & Reflection

The test proved extremely successful: dropping water purity from over 40,000 PPM down to just 12 PPM—making it significantly cleaner than regular tap water (153 PPM). It demonstrates how simple low-tech solar thermal stills can supply purified water in off-grid or emergency survival situations.
